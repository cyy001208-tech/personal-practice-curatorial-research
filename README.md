# Personal Practice Curatorial Research

> Research the practice before designing the portfolio.

`personal-practice-curatorial-research` is a Codex skill for studying a
person's completed project archive as a curatorial case. It brings finished
work, process material, firsthand testimony, collaboration records, context,
reception, contradiction, and absence into one evidence-bounded account of how
the person actually practices.

Its aim is to construct a strong and memorable interpretation without turning
the practitioner into a fictional character. The “personal mythology” produced
here is not a legend, hidden personality, or branding persona. It is a
testable explanation of demonstrated professional practice: a provisional Big
Idea supported by projects, decisions, relations, language, evidence, and
counterevidence.

The skill does not begin by selecting the most attractive projects. It begins
by asking what kinds of judgment, method, tension, and collaboration recur
across the archive—and what the archive does not allow the researcher to claim.

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Primary question** | What kind of practitioner is this person, and why is that interpretation credible? |
| **Inputs** | Completed projects, process records, firsthand testimony, collaboration evidence, context, reception, and documented gaps. |
| **Outputs** | An evidence-bounded practice assessment, competing interpretation record, Big Idea, Critical Questions, Key Messages, Theme Constellations, and optional Portfolio Chapters. |
| **Core responsibility** | Compare several plausible readings of the person before selecting, merging, juxtaposing, or withholding a thesis. |
| **Boundary** | It does not assess hidden personality, invent a heroic persona, erase collaborative authorship, or design the finished portfolio. |

## What Is Being Researched

The object of study is an already demonstrated professional practice, not an
intrinsic human essence. The research case can include:

- completed projects and final outputs;
- sketches, drafts, versions, rejected routes, and decision records;
- interviews, oral testimony, and later reflection;
- client, collaborator, and production records;
- project context and external reception;
- repeated judgments, working verbs, tensions, and relational patterns;
- contradictions, missing files, weak provenance, and absent evidence.

The archive is read across projects. A polished outcome may show what was made;
a rejected route, version difference, meeting note, or site record may show how
the person judged, negotiated, revised, or refused. Missing evidence is also a
research finding. If a project contains only publicity copy but no reliable
record of role, process, or versions, the skill marks that limit instead of
inventing a complete and persuasive project story.

The final assessment therefore applies only to practice that can be supported
by the available archive. It cannot automatically convert memory into fact,
attribute collaborative results to one person, or let an attractive future
direction rewrite what happened in earlier projects.

## Archive Work Comes Before Portfolio Structure

Source files remain in place. The research layer builds an inventory, archive
ledger, and project dossier system without treating the filesystem as a draft
table of contents.

Each project dossier distinguishes:

- the final outcome from the process that produced it;
- the person's documented role from the team's collective result;
- key decisions from later retrospective explanation;
- collaborators, clients, constraints, and production conditions;
- contemporaneous evidence from later memory;
- verified facts from hypotheses and unresolved claims;
- existing evidence from evidence that would still need to be collected.

The bundled inventory script reads metadata only and uses logical root labels
by default. A backstage manifest should not publish the host machine's absolute
paths or silently copy the archive into the Skill package.

## Competing Interpretations Come First

The first plausible description of a practitioner is often elegant,
memorable—and incomplete. Once accepted too early, it can cause every later
project to be read as confirmation. To reduce this confirmation bias, the skill
must generate two or three competing person interpretations before choosing a
thesis.

For example, the same archive might initially support different readings:

- a practitioner who translates complex content;
- a designer who coordinates complex projects and relationships;
- a spatial designer guided by visual narrative;
- a practitioner whose work is better explained by two identities held in
  productive tension.

These are not branding options. They are competing research models, and every
model must answer:

1. Which projects and source records support it?
2. Which projects can it not explain?
3. What counterevidence or resistant material remains?
4. How much unsupported inference does the interpretation require?
5. Does it preserve the person's actual role and collaborative relations?
6. Can two interpretations be merged without flattening their difference?
7. Should two identities remain visible rather than being forced into one
   label?

The result may be a selected interpretation, a merged account, coexisting
identities, or an explicitly unresolved comparison. A complex practice is not
required to collapse into a single slogan.

## The Five Research Outputs

The skill keeps five roles separate because they perform different work.

| Output | Function | What it must not become |
|---|---|---|
| **Big Idea** | The overall argument about the person's demonstrated practice. | A heroic tagline or unsupported essence. |
| **Critical Questions** | Important questions the research has not fully answered. | Claims disguised as certainty. |
| **Key Messages** | Specific judgments a reader should understand. | Repetitions of the Big Idea in slightly different words. |
| **Theme Constellations** | Projects and materials that jointly support, test, or limit a message. | Decorative categories or simple project drawers. |
| **Portfolio Chapters** | An optional first reading order derived from the research. | The research itself or a permanent classification system. |

A Theme Constellation should include not only supporting projects but also a
resistant item, exception, or unexplained edge. This keeps a theme from becoming
a self-sealing category. Portfolio Chapters are deliberately downstream-facing:
they suggest how research might be read, but they do not replace the archive,
dossiers, evidence comparison, or competing interpretations.

## Research Workflow

```text
Authorized project archive
        ↓
Inventory, archive ledger, and project dossiers
        ↓
Evidence gaps, role boundaries, and collaboration record
        ↓
Two or three competing person interpretations
        ↓
Evidence coverage, counterexample, and inference comparison
        ↓
Selected, merged, coexisting, or unresolved interpretation
        ↓
Evidence-bounded practice assessment
        ↓
Big Idea + Critical Questions + Key Messages
        ↓
Theme Constellations + optional Portfolio Chapters
```

The flow is sequential enough to protect the evidence but iterative enough to
allow later contradictions to reopen an earlier interpretation.

## What Counts as a Credible Assessment

The final assessment should explain:

- how the person can currently be understood;
- why that reading is credible;
- which projects, actions, records, and statements support it;
- which material challenges or limits it;
- where authorship is relational or shared;
- which questions remain unanswered;
- which interpretations should remain visible together;
- what additional evidence would materially change the result.

Firsthand language is situated evidence. It must retain speaker, context,
prompt, time, editorial transformation, and approval state. A later memory may
be valuable without being treated as a contemporaneous factual record.

## Boundaries

This skill evaluates demonstrated professional practice. It does not:

- assess intrinsic human worth, private character, or hidden personality;
- convert memory into verified fact;
- treat collaborative output as sole authorship;
- use desirability or future positioning to rewrite the retrospective case;
- turn every repeated motif into a permanent personal essence;
- choose projects merely because they photograph well;
- resolve missing evidence with plausible prose;
- design or implement the finished portfolio.

These limits are part of the method, not disclaimers added after the conclusion.

## Methodological Foundations

The method adapts operations from curatorial research rather than imitating an
institutional writing style.

### Modular personal worlds

[documenta 5](https://documenta.de/en/retrospective/documenta-5) contributes a
way to assemble heterogeneous objects, actions, signs, and processes into a
modular account of a person. Its history also warns that classification is an
authorial act with its own power and blind spots.

### Research platform versus public presentation

[Documenta11](https://documenta.de/en/retrospective/documenta11) helps separate
knowledge production from the final exhibition. Applied here, the archive,
testimony, project reconstruction, and interpretation comparison remain richer
than any eventual public portfolio.

### Questions across heterogeneous projects

[documenta 12](https://documenta.de/en/retrospective/documenta-12) supports the
use of sustained questions to connect projects without pretending they are the
same. [dOCUMENTA (13)](https://documenta.de/en/retrospective/documenta-13)
supports the relation between concentrated artifacts and provisional research
notes.

### Relational authorship

[documenta fifteen](https://documenta.de/en/retrospective/documenta-fifteen)
reinforces that shared infrastructure, collaboration, and relational authorship
must not disappear inside a single-person narrative.

### Audience, hierarchy, and testimony

The
[Smithsonian Guide to Exhibit Development](https://exhibits.si.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Guide-to-Exhibit-Development.pdf)
helps distinguish audience, hierarchy, Big Idea, Messages, and Questions.
[MoMA Oral History](https://www.moma.org/research/archives/oral-history) supports
situated testimony, attribution, and archival accountability rather than
context-free personal quotations.

See
[`references/curatorial-methods.md`](./personal-practice-curatorial-research/references/curatorial-methods.md)
for the full operational translation, source ledger, and methodological blind
spots.

## Handoff to Portfolio Editing

This repository answers:

> What kind of practitioner is this person, and why is that reading credible?

When the research framework is stable, hand it to
[`portfolio-exhibition-editor`](https://github.com/cyy001208-tech/portfolio-exhibition-editor).
That downstream skill answers a different question:

> How should this research be selected, written, sequenced, and constrained so
> another person can understand the practice accurately?

The downstream editor does not research “who the person is” again. It decides
what a reader encounters on first entry, how themes and projects are sequenced,
where personal language appears, what remains backstage, and how the complete
copy enters future visual or web production.

For source-heavy literature and argument reconstruction, use
[`research-space-article-ingest`](https://github.com/cyy001208-tech/CYY) before
or alongside this skill.
