# Competitive Person-Interpretation Protocol

Generate competing explanations before writing a Big Idea. Treat this as an inference-to-the-best-explanation exercise, not a branding brainstorm.

## 1. Generate two or three distinct interpretations

Assign `PI-A`, `PI-B`, and optionally `PI-C`. Give each a short functional label such as “content-translation practitioner,” “complex-project coordination designer,” or “visual-narrative spatial designer.” These examples show the naming form only; derive the actual interpretations from the corpus.

Make interpretations genuinely competitive:

- center each on a different repeated action, role, or value-producing mechanism;
- state what evidence should be visible if the interpretation is true;
- keep scope specific enough that some projects may not fit;
- avoid three flattering synonyms for “versatile creative practitioner.”

Do not write a Big Idea, final portrait, or preferred interpretation yet.

## 2. Freeze each interpretation before testing

For every `PI-#`, record:

- core proposition;
- scope in time and context;
- predicted patterns;
- supporting projects and source IDs;
- contradicting evidence;
- projects it cannot explain;
- steps of inference not directly documented;
- dependence on self-testimony, final visuals, or team outcomes;
- conditions under which it would be false.

Do not quietly broaden an interpretation after encountering a counterexample. Record the failure first.

## 3. Search asymmetrically against early preference

After generating the set:

1. identify the most intuitively attractive interpretation;
2. search for its strongest counterexample before adding more support;
3. search for the strongest overlooked evidence for the least attractive alternative;
4. inspect projects omitted from all interpretations;
5. compare self-description with process, reception, and authorship evidence.

This sequence prevents the first elegant phrase from controlling later evidence selection.

## 4. Compare on explanatory criteria

Criterion | Question
--- | ---
Evidence coverage | How many relevant projects and source types does it explain?
Unexplained remainder | Which projects or decisive materials remain outside it?
Contradiction cost | How much direct evidence must be discounted or reinterpreted?
Inference burden | How many unsupported steps are needed?
Specificity | Does it make discriminating predictions, or can it explain anything?
Temporal robustness | Does it survive across periods, or belong to one phase?
Authorship clarity | Does it depend on misattributing team output?
Holdout survival | Does it remain plausible without its strongest anchor project?

Use qualitative findings and counts, not one opaque total score. Prefer the explanation that covers consequential evidence with the fewest unsupported assumptions, not the one that sounds most impressive.

## 5. Run the holdout and negative-case tests

- Remove the strongest supporting project from each interpretation. Reassess whether the remaining archive still supports it.
- Identify one negative case for each interpretation.
- Check whether a supposedly unexplained project is genuinely contradictory, merely irrelevant, or evidence of a contextual second identity.
- Penalize an interpretation that survives only by becoming vague.

## 6. Choose one of four outcomes

### Select

Choose one interpretation when it clearly offers broader, more diverse evidence with lower inference burden and manageable counterevidence. Preserve the strongest rival in the report.

### Merge

Merge interpretations only when:

- they explain different levels or mechanisms of the same practice;
- their relationship is evidenced;
- the combined model explains more without becoming unfalsifiably broad;
- contradictions remain visible.

Do not merge merely because both are positive.

### Coexist

Retain two interpretations as parallel identities when evidence shows context-, medium-, role-, or period-dependent modes and neither can be reduced to the other. Define the boundary conditions for each and how the practitioner moves between them.

### Remain unresolved

Keep the competition open when evidence cannot discriminate. Convert the unresolved discriminator into a `CQ-##` Critical Question and specify the material or interview evidence needed.

## 7. Constrain downstream synthesis

- Draft `BI-01` only after recording the competition outcome.
- Cite the retained `PI-#` interpretation or coexistence relation under `BI-01`.
- Derive Key Messages from evidence that survives the competition.
- Preserve projects unexplained by the selected model as counterexamples, separate constellations, or Critical Questions.
- Do not delete rejected interpretations; retain their strongest evidence and rejection rationale in the research record.
