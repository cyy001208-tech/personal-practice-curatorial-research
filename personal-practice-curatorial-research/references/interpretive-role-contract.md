# Interpretive Role Contract

Keep five layers strictly separate. They form a chain of responsibility, not five ways to summarize the same material.

The competitive `PI-#` comparison is a mandatory pre-framework gate, not a sixth public-facing layer. It tests which account of the person can responsibly ground the framework.

## Role definitions

Layer | Exact job | Required form | Must not do
--- | --- | --- | ---
`BI-01` Big Idea | State the total thesis about the person's practice after the `PI-#` comparison | One contestable declarative sentence derived from a select, merge, coexist, or unresolved decision | List projects, repeat every Key Message, become a slogan, or silently choose an untested identity
`CQ-##` Critical Questions | Name questions the research has not fully answered | One to three genuine interrogative sentences tied to evidence gaps | Disguise a conclusion as a question or repeat a Key Message
`KM-##` Key Messages | State the concrete judgments the audience should understand after reading | Two to four distinct declarative statements, each evidenced | Paraphrase the Big Idea, list source material, or describe page order
`TC-##` Theme Constellations | Group projects and materials that jointly support or complicate a Key Message | A relational cluster with project/source IDs and an explicit supported `KM-##` | Introduce an unsupported new conclusion or merely name a generic skill
`CH-##` Portfolio Chapters | Translate research conclusions into reading order and transitions | An ordered sequence, each chapter mapped to `KM-##` and `TC-##` | Create new research findings or rename a Key Message as a chapter

## Dependency chain

Analyze bottom-up:

`sources → project reconstructions → cross-project codes → PI-A / PI-B / optional PI-C → counterevidence and holdout comparison → interpretation decision → candidate constellations → candidate Key Messages → BI-01 → unresolved CQ-##`

Present top-down:

`Big Idea → Critical Questions → Key Messages → Theme Constellations → Portfolio Chapters`

Portfolio Chapters are optional. The first four layers belong to the research framework. Report the `PI-#` comparison and decision in the method/evidence section before presenting that framework, or retain it in a traceable appendix when a concise public version is required.

## Traceability rules

- Do not draft `BI-01` until two or three genuinely distinct `PI-#` interpretations have been frozen, challenged, and compared.
- Record whether the result is **select**, **merge**, **coexist**, or **unresolved**. Cite the retained `PI-#` IDs under `BI-01`.
- If two identities coexist, define the contexts, media, roles, or periods in which each operates. Do not flatten them into a generic umbrella label.
- Preserve the strongest evidence for rejected interpretations as counterevidence, even when it does not appear in public-facing prose.
- Support `BI-01` with at least two distinct `KM-##` statements.
- Tie every `CQ-##` to a named gap, conflict, or missing source. If the research already answers it, remove it from Critical Questions and convert the answer into a Key Message where appropriate.
- Support every `KM-##` with one or more `TC-##` constellations.
- Populate every `TC-##` with at least two projects, unless one documented turning point justifies a single-project constellation.
- Attach precise archive source IDs to every constellation.
- Map every `CH-##` to at least one Key Message and one constellation. A chapter may stage an unresolved Critical Question, but it must not pretend to answer it.

## Anti-synonym tests

Run all tests before delivery:

1. **Competition-gate test** — if the Big Idea appeared before the `PI-#` comparison, discard it as a provisional hypothesis and rerun the comparison without privileging it.
2. **Substitution test** — if a Key Message can replace the Big Idea without losing scope, it is probably a paraphrase; narrow the Key Message.
3. **Question test** — if a Critical Question can be rewritten as “the practitioner does X” without missing evidence, it is a disguised message; move it.
4. **Evidence-container test** — if a Theme Constellation contains no project/source relationship, it is a claim, not a constellation.
5. **Sequence test** — if a Portfolio Chapter can move anywhere without changing the reading logic, its curatorial job is undefined.
6. **Same-predicate test** — Big Idea and Key Messages may share necessary nouns, but must not repeat the same subject–predicate judgment.
7. **Layer-deletion test** — deleting one layer should remove a unique function: thesis, open inquiry, audience takeaway, evidence grouping, or reading order. If nothing is lost, merge or rewrite.

## Output discipline

Use IDs in working documents and crosswalks. Remove IDs from polished public-facing prose only when traceability remains available in an appendix.

Allow important vocabulary to recur; prohibit near-identical propositions. Repetition of a term can create coherence, while repetition of a judgment creates redundancy.
