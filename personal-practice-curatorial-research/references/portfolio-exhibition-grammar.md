## Preserve the five-layer division

Read [interpretive-role-contract.md](interpretive-role-contract.md). Import the completed research hierarchy instead of rewriting it:

- `BI-01` supplies the total thesis.
- `CQ-##` records what remains unresolved; place these at moments of honest openness, not as disguised chapter claims.
- `KM-##` supplies the concrete audience takeaways.
- `TC-##` supplies the project/material relations supporting each takeaway.
- `CH-##` supplies order, pacing, and transitions.

The portfolio phase may compress language for readers, but must not create new research conclusions.

## Preserve the interpretation decision

Import the recorded `PI-#` decision alongside the five-layer framework. The comparison remains research scaffolding rather than a sixth portfolio layer, but its consequences must stay visible:

- **Select** — stage enough counterevidence to show the chosen portrait has limits.
- **Merge** — show the evidenced mechanism connecting the interpretations; do not use a vague umbrella title.
- **Coexist** — make the contexts, media, roles, or periods of both identities legible. A chapter boundary, bridge, or turning point may carry this distinction.
- **Unresolved** — let the relevant `CQ-##` remain open instead of allowing sequence or visual polish to imply a decision.

Do not remove a project merely because it supports a rival interpretation. Give it a counterexample, bridge, turning-point, or unresolved-lead role when it materially changes the portrait.

## Construct Portfolio Chapters

Give each chapter:

- an ordered `CH-##` ID;
- a short orienting title, not a rewritten Key Message;
- a unique reader task;
- mapped `KM-##` and `TC-##` IDs;
- selected projects and archive items;
- entry state, exit state, and transition.

Run the sequence test: if a chapter can move anywhere without changing the argument, define its job more precisely.

## Give projects curatorial jobs

Role | Function
--- | ---
Anchor | Proves a Key Message with dense evidence
Bridge | Links constellations, periods, or methods
Turning point | Demonstrates a change in method or position
Counterexample | Keeps the portrait from becoming falsely uniform
Context/archive | Makes another project's conditions legible
Unresolved lead | Stages a Critical Question without pretending to answer it

One project can hold multiple roles, but every included project needs at least one explicit role.

## Script the route

Use a route such as:

1. **Threshold** — state `BI-01`, subject, and time boundary.
2. **Brain** — preview decisive `TC-##` relations without explaining every project.
3. **Chapter 1** — establish the first reader takeaway.
4. **Chapter 2** — complicate or extend it through another constellation.
5. **Turning point** — change the reader's model of the practice.
6. **Counter-room** — show evidence resisting excessive coherence or supporting a retained rival interpretation.
7. **Present position** — restate what is established and expose remaining `CQ-##`.

Choose chronology only when development over time is the main thesis. Otherwise use thematic chapters while preserving dates inside them.

## Design three reading depths

Depth | Required content
--- | ---
Glance | chapter role, one Key Message, decisive image
Walk-through | constellation relation, actions, transition
Study | source evidence, process, constraints, credits, result, reflection

Do not put all evidence at the same visual weight. Keep precise documentation available without making first-pass comprehension depend on it.

## Make archive material perform

Every draft, note, timeline, quotation, or contextual item must:

- support or complicate a `KM-##`;
- establish a relation inside a `TC-##`;
- prove a decision;
- expose a tension;
- establish context;
- provide counterevidence; or
- stage an unresolved `CQ-##`.

Remove archive material used only to imply busyness or authenticity.

## Write labels as evidence

Use:

> **Object:** what the viewer is looking at.  
> **Evidence function:** which `KM-##` or `TC-##` it serves.  
> **Context:** why it existed.  
> **Consequence:** how it changes the reading.  
> **Credit:** who did what.

Keep documented fact, testimony, and interpretation distinguishable.

## Control pacing

Alternate density and pause. Use concentrated evidence near the Brain and anchors, short transitions, one deliberate friction point, visual rest before a major change, and a concise exit rather than a résumé recap.

## Test the audience route

Ask a representative reader:

- What is the total thesis about this practitioner?
- Which two or three concrete judgments did you retain?
- Which projects or materials supported each judgment?
- What remains unresolved?
- If two identities coexist, where and why does each operate?
- Why did the chapters appear in this order?
- Which sentences felt like repetitions?

Revise the information architecture before polishing visuals.
