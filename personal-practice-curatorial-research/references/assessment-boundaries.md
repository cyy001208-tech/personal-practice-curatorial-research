# Evidence-Bounded Practice Assessment

## Assess practice, not essence

Write claims about observable professional behavior within a time and corpus boundary:

- Prefer: “Across three documented projects, the practitioner repeatedly converted ambiguous briefs into staged decision systems.”
- Avoid: “The practitioner is naturally strategic.”

Do not infer intelligence, morality, mental health, protected traits, or stable personality from project artifacts.

## Separate five levels

Level | Meaning | Language
--- | --- | ---
Documented fact | Directly recorded | “The file records…” 
Corroborated pattern | Appears across projects/source types | “Across O003, P014, and R006…” 
Supported reading | Evidence favors an interpretation | “This supports reading the practice as…” 
Tentative reading | Limited or conflicting support | “One possible interpretation is…” 
Unknown | Evidence absent | “The archive does not establish…” 

## Evaluate these dimensions without a total score

- **Evidence density** — enough precise evidence supports the portrait.
- **Cross-project recurrence** — the pattern survives beyond one case.
- **Specificity** — claims describe concrete operations rather than generic praise.
- **Distinctiveness** — the pattern differentiates the practice in its real context.
- **Authorship clarity** — individual and collaborative contributions are separated.
- **Counterevidence handling** — contradictions change the interpretation.
- **Trajectory visibility** — continuity and change are both represented.
- **Audience legibility** — the intended viewer can understand the argument.
- **Curatorial coherence** — selected material serves a shared proposition without false unity.

Use `Strong`, `Moderate`, `Weak`, or `Unknown`, plus a confidence note. These diagnose the research and presentation, not the value of the person.

## Credit collaboration accurately

For every claimed capability, ask:

- Did the person execute, decide, initiate, coordinate, approve, or support?
- Which outputs were collective?
- Which resources came from the organization or collaborators?
- Is invisible coordination evidenced or merely assumed?

Name distributed authorship. Do not diminish collaborative skill, but do not convert access to a team into sole authorship.

## Preserve temporal truth

Use dates and project order. Do not:

- make later language sound as if it guided earlier work;
- treat a retrospective explanation as contemporaneous intention;
- erase abandoned methods to create a smooth progression;
- claim an inevitable career trajectory.

## Require competing interpretations

Complete [competitive-interpretation-protocol.md](competitive-interpretation-protocol.md) before final assessment. Generate two or three plausible interpretations of the same archive and compare:

- supporting evidence for each;
- projects each cannot explain;
- direct contradictions;
- unsupported inference burden;
- evidence that discriminates among them;
- whether interpretations should be selected, merged, retained as coexisting identities, or left unresolved.

If no rival interpretation is imaginable, the preferred proposition is probably too vague, too flattering, or already controlling the evidence selection. If two interpretations explain context-dependent modes without reducing to one another, preserve both instead of manufacturing a single identity.

## Keep future direction separate

The retrospective conclusion may expose unfinished questions, but it must not force a future plan. Put prospective ideas in an appendix or route them to a future-oriented research workflow after the current portrait is locked.
